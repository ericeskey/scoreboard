# 02 — Framing

*Move 2 of the post-encounter workflow. What problem statements, hypotheses, and points of view are important to PSI?*

## What is actually at stake for PSI

The May 18 meeting changed our picture of what PSI is solving for. The pre-meeting memo treated PSI's problem as *prioritization uncertainty* — too many promising indications, no clear first wedge. That framing is still true, but it is now one layer inside a larger problem that surfaced only when Julia and Bill spoke for themselves.

The deeper problem is **structural commercialization incapacity at the firm level**. PSI is an S-corp, cannot take outside capital, has no internal commercialization team, and has historically reached for low-cost market-assessment consultants (Wellesley Associates, Igenico/Adam Ehrlich) inside TABA-scale budgets to fill the gap one technology at a time. The wedge question is a symptom; the underlying condition is that PSI invents faster than its structure permits it to commercialize. Every individual technology decision compounds against the same constraint.

This reframes the engagement. DHW is not being asked only to pick a wedge. We are being asked, implicitly, to bring a commercialization apparatus that PSI cannot build internally. Bill's "open the aperture" instinct is consistent with that — if commercialization muscle is what's missing, looking at two technologies is the wrong unit of analysis; looking at the portfolio is closer to right.

## Problem statements important to PSI

### As PSI would (probably) state them

1. **"We have more good technologies than we can pursue. We need help knowing which to bet on."** This is the surface problem and the easiest to fund. It maps to TABA-scale work and is how PSI has historically scoped this kind of engagement.

2. **"We don't have the muscle to take a technology from lab to durable commercial adoption."** Julia named this. Nick's "valley of death" framing on May 7 named it. It is the problem PSI knows it has but does not have a template for solving inside its current structure.

3. **"Our structure (S-corp, ESOP) limits our ability to raise capital directly, so commercializable technologies require a spin-out path we don't have repeatable experience with."** Bill named this. It is the most structurally consequential problem on the table and was new information to DHW.

4. **"We have used outside help before for this kind of work and the results were mixed. We need to decide whether to spend more (PSI dollars, not SBIR/TABA) for a deeper engagement, and we don't yet know if that's worth it."** This is the cost-justification problem. It is the question Bill and Julia will be asking each other in their internal conversation.

### As DHW would re-state them (the framing move)

5. **"PSI has succeeded scientifically but has not yet built a commercialization function. The decision in front of PSI's executive team is not which technology to commercialize first, it is whether to invest in building (or renting) a commercialization apparatus before another technology cycle compounds the problem."** This is the reframe DHW has earned the right to test, and the one the pre-meeting memo gestures at but does not name this directly.

6. **"The single highest-leverage move for PSI is to choose a first commercial wedge with enough conviction to commit organizational resources to it — not because the wedge itself is the prize, but because the act of choosing forces PSI to build the commercialization muscle it lacks. The wedge is the training ground for the apparatus."** This is the more ambitious version of the same reframe. It connects the prioritization problem to the structural problem and gives PSI a reason to act now rather than later.

## Hypotheses

### Hypotheses DHW is carrying into the next phase

H1. The constraint is no longer technical uncertainty for DermVisio and TVisio; it is prioritization plus organizational commitment.

H2. PSI's executive team is not yet aligned on whether commercialization is a portfolio question (multiple wedges, sequenced, requiring apparatus) or a single-technology question (pick one, see what happens). The May 18 conversation hinted that Bill is closer to the portfolio view and Julia is closer to the single-technology view; Nick has been operating one technology at a time.

H3. The S-corp / spin-out constraint is more consequential than DHW initially weighted. Any commercialization path that requires outside capital forces a spin-out, which forces PSI to confront whether it has the leadership, governance, and operational capacity to stand up a spin-out — a question separate from the technology itself.

H4. PSI's prior outside-help experiences (Wellesley, Igenico) anchored their mental price-and-depth model on TABA-scale work. DHW will be priced against that anchor whether or not the work is comparable. The pre-meeting memo's hybrid (fee + milestone upside) is partly an answer to this; it shifts the comparison from "TABA consultant rate" to "outcome-aligned partner."

H5. Bill's "very small sliver" comment plus the case examples we shared in the call have already widened PSI's conception of DHW's scope beyond the two named technologies. The internal conversation will, with non-trivial probability, return to us with a request to "look across the portfolio first, then narrow."

### Hypotheses worth pressure-testing in the next phase

H6. The right first deliverable is not a wedge recommendation. It is a portfolio map plus a small number of candidate wedges with the apparatus assumptions made explicit. This lets PSI choose with eyes open rather than commit to a wedge in isolation.

H7. The right structural conversation for PSI is not "should we do a spin-out for DermVisio" but "what is our spin-out playbook, and who runs it." DHW can shape that conversation but cannot resolve it for PSI — leadership identity questions live inside PSI.

H8. There is a non-zero chance that the highest-value first engagement is not on DermVisio or TVisio at all, but on a technology that didn't surface in the May 18 conversation — one that better matches a near-term commercial event DHW can plausibly cause.

## Points of view

POV 1. **DHW is being evaluated on fit and presence as much as on method.** "Personal chemistry is #1." The site visit matters. Daniel and David being local matters. Eric showing up in person eventually will matter. The methodological case has been made; the remaining case is human.

POV 2. **The cost differential question is real and needs to be made explicit, not avoided.** PSI's anchor is TABA / Wellesley / Igenico — single-person, low-cost, mixed-result. DHW's engagement is a different unit and a different price. Better to name that delta in the next conversation than to let it sit unresolved while Bill and Julia weigh it privately.

POV 3. **The pre-meeting memo's Option B (fee plus milestone upside) is the right structure for this specific situation, and the May 18 conversation strengthens that view.** Milestones tied to spin-out events, partner introductions, licensee LOIs, or non-dilutive funding triggers all map directly to what PSI cannot do internally — they pay for the apparatus, not the activity.

POV 4. **The portfolio question is the right next move, but DHW should not preempt PSI's internal alignment.** If PSI's executive team comes back having decided to broaden scope on their own, that is durable. If DHW pushes the broadening, it is a sale. Wait for the internal conversation; respond to what they bring back.

POV 5. **The deeper engagement DHW should aim for, if PSI elects to go forward, is to build PSI's commercialization apparatus as a recurring capability — not to run one engagement and walk away.** This is consistent with how Daniel described the Teleflex Interventional Cardiology incubator-accelerator in the pre-meeting memo. It is also the structure most aligned with PSI's underlying problem.

- A short reflection on the prior-consultant comparison and why DHW's structure is a different unit of work, framed honestly rather than defensively.
