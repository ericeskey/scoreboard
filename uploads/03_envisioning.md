# 03 — Envisioning

*Move 3 of the post-encounter workflow. Concretize a specific future state without prematurely solving.*

## What are we aiming for here?

There are two future states worth concretizing, at two different time horizons. The May 18 conversation conflated them; the envisioning move separates them.

### Future state at the engagement-design horizon (3–6 months out)

We are aiming for an engagement with PSI that is **structurally honest about what PSI actually needs and what DHW can actually do**. Not a wedge study. Not a market assessment. A focused first phase whose deliverable is a shared, executive-team-endorsed answer to two questions:

1. Which one or two technologies in PSI's portfolio (which may or may not be DermVisio and TVisio) does PSI want to commit organizational resources to commercializing first, and why?
2. What is the spin-out path, or the non-spin-out alternative path, for that technology — and is PSI willing and able to walk it?

The output is not a deck. It is a **clean choice** that PSI's executive team can either commit to or consciously set aside. Either outcome is success — committing to commercialization is success because PSI moves; consciously setting aside is success because PSI stops paying the cost of pretending it might.

### Future state at the relationship horizon (12–24 months out)

If the first phase lands well, the aspirational future state is **DHW operating as PSI's external commercialization apparatus**, structured similarly to the Teleflex Interventional Cardiology incubator-accelerator engagement: a fee for the strategic apparatus we build and run, with milestone-triggered upside for the commercial events the work materially causes.

This future state has DHW running a repeatable function inside PSI's strategic process: portfolio review, wedge selection, market evidence build, partner/licensee/spin-out routing, investor materials, founding-team and external-CEO sourcing where spin-out is the path. PSI does not need to build this capability internally because it has built it externally with us.

This is the engagement we aspire to. It is also the one we've earned by demonstrating that the first phase produces a clean choice PSI can act on.

## What will it look like when they get there?

### At the engagement-design horizon

- A shared problem statement, written down, that Nick, Julia, and Bill agree describes PSI's actual problem (not the surface problem of which-tech-first, but the underlying problem of building or renting a commercialization apparatus before another invention cycle compounds it).
- A portfolio map of PSI's commercializable technologies, with the apparatus assumptions made explicit per candidate. Not 20 technologies in detail — a ranked short list with the bets named.
- One or two named first-phase candidate technologies with a clear case for each: what the wedge is, what evidence is needed, what spin-out (or non-spin-out) path the wedge enables.
- A signed engagement structure (Option B, fee + milestone-triggered upside) with co-defined milestones, a causation test, and a light governance cadence (quarterly milestone review).
- A working-session calendar for the first 90 days of engagement, with named participants, deliverables, and decision gates.

### At the relationship horizon

- DHW is in PSI's strategic process as a recurring partner, not as an episodic consultant. There is a meeting cadence; there are shared documents; there is institutional memory inside DHW about PSI's portfolio and inside PSI about DHW's method.
- At least one PSI technology has moved from "promising portfolio asset" to "actively commercializing toward a defined endpoint" — partner-engaged, LOI-or-better signed, spin-out chartered, or licensee in late-stage negotiation. The category of milestone matters less than the fact that the milestone is real.
- PSI's executive team is having different internal conversations than they were having before DHW arrived. They are asking different questions in portfolio reviews. The framing has propagated.
- DHW has been paid against milestones that were observably caused by the work, not just against retainer for activity. Option B is structurally vindicated rather than theoretically defended.
- Bill, Julia, and Nick refer DHW to other organizations in their network (PSI peers, federally-funded translational programs, corporate innovation groups) because the engagement has been transformative enough that they want others to have access to it.

## How will we know we are there?

### Signals at the engagement-design horizon

- The site visit happens within the mid-June window or shortly after.
- The site-visit working session produces an explicit decision to enter a first phase, not another exploratory conversation.
- Bill or Julia (not just Nick) names the engagement structure conversation as something they want to have, not something they want to delay.
- A signed engagement is in place within 60 days of the site visit.
- The signed engagement names milestones, not just deliverables. Milestones are observable, dated, and tied to commercial events DHW can plausibly cause.

### Signals at the relationship horizon

- PSI brings the next technology to DHW unprompted, before SBIR cycle ends, with the question "should this go into the portfolio review."
- Bill or Julia volunteers DHW to a peer organization.
- A spin-out is chartered or a license is executed against work DHW shaped. The milestone payment is straightforward to validate; the causation test does not require lawyering.
- DHW's case-study deck gains a PSI case alongside Teleflex UroLift, Roche/Neurify, and Teleflex Interventional Cardiology. PSI is willing to be referenced.

## What we are explicitly NOT aiming for

Some envisioning moves require managing expectations down. A few worth naming for ourselves:

- **We are not aiming to be PSI's commercialization team in-residence.** Even if PSI lacks internal commercialization muscle, DHW is not the answer to that absence at the operational level — we are the answer at the strategic-apparatus level. If the engagement drifts into "DHW runs commercialization," we've miscoped.
- **We are not aiming to make PSI investable as PSI.** The S-corp constraint is real. The aspiration is to make individual PSI technologies investable via spin-out (or licensable via partner), not to transform PSI's corporate structure.
- **We are not aiming to take equity-style risk on PSI's commercialization outcomes.** Option C in the pre-meeting memo names why. The engagement is advisory with milestone upside, not co-venture. If PSI wants co-venture, that's a different conversation with a different vehicle, and we'd revisit it on its own terms.
- **We are not aiming to win every technology in PSI's portfolio for engagement.** A short list, then a focused first phase, then expansion based on demonstrated value. The aperture-widening conversation belongs to PSI; DHW responds, doesn't push.

## What still needs to be decided before we commit

- Whether PSI's spin-out muscle is dormant (a routing condition, executable when needed) or structurally absent (a stop condition that changes the engagement design). Tested in the site visit per A5 in the assumptions file.
- Whether the cost-and-depth differential between DHW and PSI's prior consultants is acceptable to Bill and Julia when they have the conversation without DHW in the room. Tested by the callback timing and tone per A6 and A8.
- Whether the right first focus is DermVisio + TVisio or a different technology in the portfolio. Tested by PSI's own top-three ranking when they do the internal conversation per A4.

The envisioning move does not resolve these. It names what success would look like *given* a path through them, so we know what we are aiming at when we walk in.
