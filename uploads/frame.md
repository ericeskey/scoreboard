# FRAME: What the work is (client-ready source for Claude Design)

PAGE INTENT: Candidate framings of the work, each affirmable or correctable. Every framing traces to named voices from the Listen page, so affirming a frame is affirming the people who supplied it.

PAGE HEADLINE: What the work is. Seven framings, each built from your organization's own words.
PAGE SUBTEXT: The direction is already ratified: five recommendations, accepted. These framings define the shape of what comes next. React to each.

## CARD F1: The quarterly is a decision forum, not a smaller monthly
BODY: The monthly was a report: activity numbers, an all-levels room, no questions asked, unnoticed when paused. The quarterly earns existence only by ending in decisions that cannot be made anywhere else.
EXPAND: The survey's verdict was on the form, not the function. The old monthly put PMO coordinators in front of presidents, and almost nobody asked questions. Ron Aplin asks for cross-BU and enterprise issues handled by executive leaders. Matthew James says the forum is worth attending where material business risk requires decision or agreement. The directors decline to attend another status meeting. All three point the same direction. The positioning consequence: the quarterly should never be sold as restored visibility, because nobody misses the visibility. It is the room for what the coping layer cannot decide.

## CARD F2: The scope is bounded and countable
BODY: 24 projects span two or more RemainCo BUs. 14 span RemainCo and NewCo. About 5% of the portfolio. That population, plus enterprise trends and capacity, is the quarterly's whole territory.
EXPAND: Andrej Baranek's challenge (why does anything need to exist above BU reviews?) sets the boundary condition. The answer is the cross-BU population only an enterprise room can govern: roughly 38 projects today, plus the enterprise-level reads (direction, risk, capacity) that no single BU review produces. Small enough to be credible, real enough to matter. Anything broader re-becomes the monthly and fails Andrej's test. The same discipline applies to the scoreboard itself: Matt's experience says a dashboard survives at 3 to 5 headline measures, not 10.

## CARD F3: The coping layer is an asset to elevate, not a gap to fix
BODY: Hannah's group, the Multi-BU Intake forum, and ad-hoc escalation work well. The quarterly should receive only what that machinery cannot absorb.
EXPAND: The survey proves the operational machinery works: cross-BU projects are managed, forums get created when needed, escalation paths function. The design should ride on this rather than duplicate it. Directors and the portfolio-management group feed the dashboard and surface exceptions. The quarterly receives the residue: cross-BU priority conflicts, capacity versus margin trade-offs, systemic barriers needing a common approach, and attention-contention events of the Lidocaine class. Framed this way, the quarterly relieves the operational layer of silently absorbing enterprise problems. It does not audit that layer.

## CARD F4: Two clocks, by design
BODY: Clock A: automate the base data. Clock B: design the quarterly in parallel so its first run reports what the automation produces. Nobody waits on plumbing to start.
EXPAND: This is Matt's own architecture. The automation lane (online intake form, automated collection and compilation, Power BI and SharePoint) is the prerequisite for everything and is already in motion. The quarterly's first iteration is designed to run on exactly what that lane produces: a credible better-worse-or-no-change story from data that exists today. Richer measures arrive on a visible roadmap: each quarter, the meeting's own agenda includes what improved in how we measure. The financial destination (cost of sustaining, revenue at risk) waits on one piece of plumbing, the SKU-to-IPN-to-SAP linkage, proven first in whichever BU's data is closest. Surgical's is in hand.

## CARD F5: The truth fracture: expose it on the instrument, don't broker it
BODY: Financial truth lives in Planview. Project truth lives in four BU tools. They do not reconcile, and the drift produces claims like savings on canceled projects. The scoreboard must show which measures wait on this decision.
EXPAND: Planview carries the cost-improvement financials, maintained by procurement. Project status lives in Power BI, Smartsheet, Microsoft Lists, and monday.com depending on the BU. The two are not synced, and analysis has caught savings being credited against projects that were canceled or on hold. The fix (choose the source of truth for project status) is a governance decision nobody has been willing to make. The design move: make each KPI's dependence on that decision visible on the instrument itself. This measure is live; this one waits on the source-of-truth call. That converts an unowned question into a named item that leadership can arbitrate in the decisions section of the quarterly. The meeting becomes the forcing function for the decision no one could force alone.

## CARD F6: The KPI cut is discovered, not debated
BODY: Which 3 to 5 measures make the headline? Not the ones anyone argues for. The ones that prove themselves against real data.
EXPAND: The last two attempts at a sustaining scoreboard died the same way: a 10-KPI dashboard was rejected as too much, and an earlier financial-KPI proposal was presented and never landed. Both were designed by judgment and defended by argument. The alternative: test all twelve candidate measures against the data that actually exists (the survey, the INTV dashboard, the SKU files, Planview extracts), rank them by evidence strength, and let the top 3 to 5 emerge. The list then defends itself in front of any executive: this measure is on the dashboard because its data is clean end to end; this one is on the roadmap because it waits on the IPN linkage. The answer is empirical rather than anyone's taste, and it survives drilling.

## CARD F7: Socialization is the design method
BODY: The accepted recommendations were assembled from stakeholders' own words, and it worked. The quarterly's design extends the same method deliberately.
EXPAND: Every element of the proposed design traces to a named voice: Ron supplied the decisions agenda, Matthew James the cadence and content gate, Sara the dashboard-plus-quarterly architecture, Jake the headline question, Nishith the capacity gate, Andrej the boundary test, the directors the attendance rule. Socializing the design means showing people their own words, organized. This also resolves the room question: the survey respondents are the natural candidates for the quarterly room, each arriving with a stake they stated themselves.

## CARD F8: What this work is not
BODY: Not a tooling project. Not the automation lane. Not an audit of the operational layer. Not an enterprise data-governance crusade.
EXPAND: The tooling conversation gets easy once what to measure and what to decide are settled; it stays downstream. The automation lane is already owned and moving. The operational coping layer works and should be elevated, not re-examined. And the source-of-truth fracture gets exposed on the instrument for leadership to arbitrate, not brokered from below. Keeping these out is what keeps the work small enough to succeed.
