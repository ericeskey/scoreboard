# 04 — Crux Analysis

*Move 4 of the post-encounter workflow. Challenge-based strategy. What is the pivotal solvable problem? What reframings are possible, which is most effective?*

## Applying the Crux Guide to PSI

### Diagnosis

PSI presents as a wedge-selection problem: too many promising indications, no clear first commercialization target. That is the surface problem and the easiest to fund. Under Rumelt's discipline, we do not start with the surface problem — we ask what the *pivotal solvable challenge* is, the one that once solved untangles the bigger ones.

The May 18 conversation surfaced three candidate diagnoses. Each generates a different crux. The discipline of the move is to evaluate which crux, if solved, actually untangles the others.

**Candidate diagnosis A — Wedge-selection.** PSI has multiple mature optical platforms and cannot afford to pursue all simultaneously. The first wedge has not been selected with enough conviction to commit organizational resources.

**Candidate diagnosis B — Commercialization-apparatus absence.** PSI invents faster than it commercializes because it has no internal commercialization function and no repeatable playbook for partnering, licensing, or spinning out. Wedge selection is downstream of this — even with a perfect wedge, PSI cannot execute on it without the apparatus.

**Candidate diagnosis C — Structural-channel constraint.** PSI's S-corp / ESOP structure forbids direct outside investment, channeling any venture-capital path through spin-outs. PSI has minimal repeatable experience with spin-outs. The constraint shapes every commercialization decision.

Each diagnosis points at a different crux. Each crux is a different ASC (Addressable Strategic Challenge).

## Five candidate reframings (cruxes)

### Crux 1 — "Pick the first wedge"

*Statement.* The pivotal challenge is selecting one technology (DermVisio, TVisio, or a portfolio peer) as the first commercialization target with enough evidence that PSI's executive team will commit resources.

*Solvable with existing resources?* Yes. This is what TABA-scale work and prior consultants attempted. The method exists. The ASC is well-defined.

*If solved, what untangles?* The immediate question — "what do we work on now" — gets answered. PSI can deploy capital and attention against a single target.

*If solved, what does NOT untangle?* The commercialization-apparatus problem persists. PSI still lacks the muscle to execute on the wedge once selected. The next technology cycle re-creates the same wedge-selection problem. The S-corp constraint still routes anything venture-funded through a spin-out PSI does not know how to execute.

*Critique.* This is the surface problem dressed as a crux. Rumelt's warning applies: solving this leaves the deeper structure intact. PSI has done versions of this before (Wellesley, Igenico); the results were mixed; the underlying condition did not change.

### Crux 2 — "Build a repeatable wedge-selection capability"

*Statement.* The pivotal challenge is building, in PSI, a repeatable process for evaluating its portfolio, ranking candidate wedges, and committing resources — so that the wedge question never sits unresolved again.

*Solvable with existing resources?* Partially. Requires DHW or equivalent to teach the method, requires PSI executive time to internalize it. Doable but expensive in attention.

*If solved, what untangles?* Future portfolio decisions get cleaner. The institutional muscle improves. PSI starts asking the right questions in portfolio reviews independent of DHW's presence.

*If solved, what does NOT untangle?* The commercialization-apparatus problem persists at the execution layer. PSI now knows what to commercialize but still doesn't have the function to commercialize it. The wedge gets picked correctly and then dies in the valley of death anyway. The S-corp constraint is still untouched.

*Critique.* Better than Crux 1 because it builds durable capability. Still incomplete — the apparatus problem and the structural problem are not addressed. This is the "teach to fish" frame applied only to the upstream half of the problem.

### Crux 3 — "Stand up the commercialization apparatus"

*Statement.* The pivotal challenge is building or renting the external commercialization function PSI cannot build internally — a repeatable apparatus for moving selected technologies from PSI's lab toward partner-ready, licensee-ready, or spin-out-ready form, with the strategic muscle to do this multiple times rather than once.

*Solvable with existing resources?* Yes, if PSI engages DHW (or an equivalent) in the apparatus posture, not the wedge-study posture. The Teleflex Interventional Cardiology engagement is the proof of concept. The resources are PSI's executive attention and the milestone-aligned investment in DHW.

*If solved, what untangles?* The wedge-selection problem gets handled inside the apparatus. The commercialization-execution problem gets handled inside the apparatus. The S-corp / spin-out problem becomes a routing decision inside the apparatus rather than a structural blocker. Future technology cycles plug into the same apparatus rather than re-creating the same problem each time.

*If solved, what does NOT untangle?* PSI's identity as an inventing organization stays intact; the apparatus is external. If DHW becomes indispensable, PSI's dependence increases — which is the "teach to fish" tension. Also: the S-corp constraint is routed around, not resolved. If PSI's ESOP holders or board want different outcomes than the apparatus produces (faster cash, no spin-outs), the apparatus cannot deliver them.

*Critique.* This is the most consequential crux. It addresses all three diagnoses simultaneously: wedge selection, execution muscle, and structural routing. It also matches what DHW is best at and most aspires to. The risk is scope-creep — calling this the crux can be self-serving for DHW. We must hold ourselves accountable to Rumelt's "honest diagnosis" discipline.

### Crux 4 — "Resolve the spin-out playbook"

*Statement.* The pivotal challenge is building PSI's repeatable spin-out playbook — leadership identification, governance, equity distribution, ESOP-holder alignment, founding-team sourcing — so that when a wedge is sharp enough to commercialize, the structural channel is ready rather than improvised.

*Solvable with existing resources?* Partially. Requires PSI leadership commitment and likely outside legal/governance expertise beyond DHW's typical scope. Spin-out playbooks are sector-specific and idiosyncratic.

*If solved, what untangles?* The structural constraint becomes a feature rather than a blocker. Any future wedge can route through a proven channel. ESOP-holder concerns get addressed in template rather than per-deal.

*If solved, what does NOT untangle?* Wedge selection is still unresolved. The commercialization-apparatus is still missing — knowing how to spin out doesn't tell you what to spin out, or whether to spin out vs. license vs. partner.

*Critique.* This is too far downstream. You cannot build the spin-out playbook before you know what you'd spin out. Crux 4 is a *sub-crux* inside Crux 3 — a piece of the apparatus, not a standalone crux.

### Crux 5 — "Frame the choice before structure"

*Statement.* The pivotal challenge is sharpening the problem statement PSI's executive team holds, so that the engagement design (apparatus vs. wedge study, spin-out vs. license vs. partner, focused vs. portfolio) flows from clarity rather than from each individual's anchored mental model.

*Solvable with existing resources?* Yes, and cheaply. A working session, a portfolio-level frame document, and an honest cost-and-depth conversation can be done in one or two meetings.

*If solved, what untangles?* The executive team is aligned on what they're actually buying when they engage DHW. Bill, Julia, and Nick stop carrying three different problem statements. The right engagement design becomes self-evident from the right problem statement.

*If solved, what does NOT untangle?* Nothing in the underlying problem changes — but the *path to working on it* gets unblocked. Crux 5 is the meta-crux: the unlock that lets the real work begin.

*Critique.* This is the move that has to happen first regardless of which other crux is right. It's a precondition more than a crux itself. Rumelt would say: if you cannot get the diagnosis right, no crux is reachable. Crux 5 is the diagnosis step.

## Rank order by effectiveness

Effectiveness = the degree to which solving this crux unlocks the bigger structure, plus solvability with available resources, minus the risk of self-serving framing.

**1. Crux 3 — Stand up the commercialization apparatus.** Most consequential. Addresses all three diagnoses. Matches DHW's best work. The honesty test: this is also what DHW most aspires to engage on, which is exactly the kind of self-serving frame Rumelt warns about. We should hold it as the right crux *only if* PSI's executive team independently arrives near the same diagnosis. If we have to push them into it, we are diagnosing for DHW's preferences, not PSI's reality.

**2. Crux 5 — Frame the choice before structure.** The precondition. Has to happen first. Cheap, fast, high-leverage. Does not require commitment from PSI beyond a working session. This is what the site visit should actually be. The risk is that PSI treats it as another introduction rather than a diagnostic working session; we mitigate by scoping the site visit explicitly toward this outcome in the recap email.

**3. Crux 2 — Build repeatable wedge-selection capability.** A middle option that improves PSI durably without requiring the larger apparatus commitment. If Crux 3 is too ambitious for the first engagement, Crux 2 is the responsible step-down. It builds capability that is still useful later if PSI ever does engage in apparatus mode.

**4. Crux 1 — Pick the first wedge.** The surface problem. Solvable but insufficient. Worth doing only as a deliverable inside a larger engagement (Crux 2 or 3), never as the engagement itself. PSI has done this version before; doing it again with DHW would change the consultant but not the outcome.

**5. Crux 4 — Resolve the spin-out playbook.** Not standalone. A component of Crux 3 if it becomes relevant. Premature as a top-line crux.

## What this implies for next moves

- **The site visit's job is to do Crux 5.** Sharpen the problem statement together, expose the three candidate diagnoses honestly, see which one Bill, Julia, and Nick recognize as theirs.
- **The first engagement's job is Crux 2 or Crux 3, depending on what surfaces in Crux 5.** If PSI's executive team recognizes the apparatus diagnosis, propose Crux 3 (apparatus engagement, Teleflex-style). If they recognize the wedge-selection diagnosis but not the apparatus diagnosis, propose Crux 2 (a focused first phase that builds wedge-selection capability while delivering on DermVisio/TVisio or whatever short list emerges).
- **Do not lead with Crux 3.** Even if it's the right answer, leading with it looks like DHW maximizing scope. Let PSI arrive at it. The pre-meeting memo already did the work of putting the apparatus frame in front of them; the site visit's job is to see whether it landed.
- **Do not accept Crux 1 as the engagement scope.** If PSI comes back asking only for the wedge study, the right move is to reframe to Crux 2 (same deliverable, capability transfer included) rather than to discount-price a Crux 1 deliverable. Discount-pricing Crux 1 trains PSI that DHW is interchangeable with Wellesley, which is exactly the cost-comparison frame we want to defuse.
