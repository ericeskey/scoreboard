## SECTION: STATE A: The first quarterly EPR

INTRO CARD
BODY: One hour. A deliberately designed room. Five beats. It ends in decisions.
EXPAND: The room is designed, not accreted: the survey respondents are the natural candidates, each with a stated stake, with final seats confirmed with Harsh on his return. Directors are deliberately not in it; the dashboard serves them, and the quarterly consumes only exceptions. The five beats below each carry the name of the person whose stated need they answer.

### BEAT CARDS (the agenda; carry over the existing Blueprint agenda reactions)

BEAT 1: Portfolio health headline (5 min)
BODY: Better, worse, or no change. Four domains, trend arrows against last quarter, one slide. Jake Newman's question, answered first.
EXPAND: The direction answer lands before anything else gets discussed. This is the question the function was created to answer and never could while project counts were the only measure. The four domains and their candidate measures appear in the KPI band below.

BEAT 2: What's coming (15 min)
BODY: By exception only: projects trending to trouble, supplier and obsolescence exposure, compliance-linked strain. History stays in the appendix.
EXPAND: Anticipation over history. This beat gives events of the Lidocaine class (attention consumed in one place silently costing a decision somewhere else) a surface where they appear before they cost a quarter. Cory O'Rorke's story is the worked example of what this beat exists to catch.

BEAT 3: Capacity and margin (15 min)
BODY: How much sustaining capacity keeps the lights on, how much is free for margin work, and what that means for taking on more.
EXPAND: Nishith Desai's question made measurable: prioritization means nothing while everything stays active. The capacity gate is what converts priority labels into real sequencing. This is also the value-creation read Harsh asked for: sustaining as capacity for margin improvement, with an honest account of how much is spoken for.

BEAT 4: Decisions needed from leadership (15 min)
BODY: Explicit asks: cross-BU priority conflicts, the source-of-truth call, resource trade-offs. The beat that makes the meeting un-pausable.
EXPAND: Ron Aplin's specification, operating. Leaders attend because only they can do what this beat does. The old monthly demanded nothing of its room and got nothing from it; its pause went unnoticed for six months. This beat is the structural inverse. The source-of-truth question (which record governs project status) is the standing first candidate for this beat's docket.

BEAT 5: What's changing in how we measure (10 min)
BODY: The roadmap moving: which data linkage advanced, which measure graduated to live. Leadership watches the scoreboard improve on a schedule.
EXPAND: Every quarter, the meeting reports on its own instrument: SKU-to-IPN-to-SAP beachhead progress, measures moving from the build column to the live column. Harsh sees the financial measures approaching rather than being promised.

### KPI BAND (carry over the existing Blueprint KPI cards and their reactions, reframed under State A)

BAND INTRO
BODY: Twelve candidate measures in four domains. The headline set of 3 to 5 is discovered, not debated: each candidate is tested against real data, and the survivors carry their own evidence.
EXPAND: Each measure is tagged live-now (reachable from data that exists and reconciles) or build (waits on a named dependency such as the IPN linkage or the source-of-truth decision). The evidence pass on the crux page is what performs this test. The domains: Direction and Delivery (Jake's question), Risk and Anticipation (what's coming), Capacity and Value (Nishith's gate), Financial (the destination: cost of sustaining and revenue at risk, arriving via the beachhead BU).

### STATE A SUCCESS MARKERS CARD
BODY: A VP asks an unprompted question. At least one decision is minuted per quarter. Every headline KPI survives drilling. Directors are visibly better off out of the room.
EXPAND: The old monthly's pathology was silence: an all-levels room where nobody asked anything. The first two runs of the quarterly are credible if the room engages on its own, if beat 4 produces minuted decisions, if each headline measure can answer "why is this one here" with evidence, and if the directors experience the change as a dashboard that serves them rather than a meeting that summons them.

### STATE A NOT-AIMING-FOR CARD
BODY: No raw project counts as headline. No restarted monthly in disguise. No duplicate of the intake forums. No financial ROI in quarter one. No enterprise-wide source-of-truth resolution as a precondition.
EXPAND: Project counts are the measure everyone already rejects. The quarterly is a different instrument from the monthly, not a lower frequency of it. Hannah's group and the Multi-BU Intake forum keep doing what they do well. The financial measures arrive via the roadmap, not by promise. And the source-of-truth fracture needs resolving only for the measures on the page; the rest is beat 4 material.

## SECTION: STATE B: The measurement system a year out

CARD B1: The data foundation runs itself
BODY: Intake form live, collection automated, Power BI and SharePoint carrying all portfolio reporting. The month-end scramble no longer depends on any one person.
EXPAND: This is the automation lane completed: the base data (counts, status, basics) flows without manual compilation. The vulnerability exposed this year (one departure, and reporting nearly stopped) is closed structurally.

CARD B2: The dollars arrive
BODY: The SKU-to-IPN-to-SAP linkage proven in one BU and extending. Cost of sustaining and revenue at risk reported in dollars, trended like everything else.
EXPAND: The financial measures leadership has wanted for two years finally get plumbing instead of promises. Surgical's SKU data is the closest starting point; the command center and the IPN owner are the institutional assets the linkage runs through. The earlier financial-KPI vision was right; it lacked this foundation.

CARD B3: Absorbed work becomes visible as managed load
BODY: Recurrence, absorbed impacts, and at-risk exposure ride on reconciled records. Quiet coping shows up as managed load instead of disappearing into false health.
EXPAND: Expedites, exclude-and-ship events, cross-training around down lines: today this coping erases the very signals a scoreboard needs. On reconciled records, it becomes an early-warning read. The organization's skill at absorbing problems stays; its invisibility cost goes.

CARD B4: The BU layer is legible without another meeting
BODY: Consistent BU-review guidelines make the BU layer report upward cleanly. Andrej's alternative, absorbed into the design.
EXPAND: Nishith asked for consistency in how BU portfolio reviews run. Andrej suggested consistent reporting expectations might do the enterprise job. Both are right, and both are satisfied by the same artifact: BU-review guidelines that feed the dashboard, leaving the quarterly to decide only what BU reviews cannot.

CARD B5: The function answers its founding question
BODY: Sustaining's story changes register: from counting activity to governing exposure and capacity.
EXPAND: The function Jake Newman created finally answers the question he created it to answer, on a quarterly cadence, in front of the leaders who need it, with the evidence attached.

## SECTION: STATE C: The work between (leads into the commit page)

CARD C1: Phase 1: the evidence pass
BODY: Four to six weeks. Test the twelve candidates against real data. Deliver the defensible 3-to-5 cut, the reachability map, the first quarterly's design, and the socialization set in stakeholders' own words.
EXPAND: The bounded piece of work the crux names. Inputs already in hand or arriving: two survey waves, the INTV dashboard, Surgical's SKU file, Planview extracts. It runs alongside the automation lane and is built to relieve load: synthesis is absorbed, artifacts arrive forwardable, nothing requires rewriting. Output on the table at the end: a first quarterly EPR that can be scheduled with confidence.

CARD C2: Phase 2: named, not scoped
BODY: The build: dashboard views extended across BUs, the financial-linkage beachhead proven, the build-column measures brought live.
EXPAND: Phase 2 exists so Phase 1 can stay small. It is scoped only after the evidence pass shows what the data justifies. Sara's ask (the same views for the rest of the organization) and the IPN beachhead are its natural centerpieces.

CARD C3: The sequence
BODY: Socialize this record. Shape the paper. Run the evidence pass while the automation lane builds. Land the first quarterly with evidence attached. Then build what the evidence justified.
EXPAND: Each step is cheap relative to the next and de-risks it. The socialization uses this site: every stakeholder sees their own words in the design. The paper conversation belongs this week or next, so the direction and the paper arrive together before Harsh returns.
