## Compact reflection: what was said

The meeting was a first-time introduction to PSI's executive layer (Julia Dupuis, EVP Optical Systems; Bill Kessler, VP Applied Optics) following Nick Iftimia's May 7 conversation with DHW. Nick set the agenda as mutual introduction plus a discussion of how DHW typically evaluates technologies and works with companies. He had not yet briefed Bill or Julia on specifics — they were meeting DHW cold.

DHW opened with a layered self-introduction: Daniel's bench-to-market trajectory (six startups, Harvard/Brown tech transfer, ARMI/HHS Israeli accelerator, the Gary Magnith connection), Eric's engineer-to-discovery arc grounded in 20 years of demand-discovery practice, and David Potter's healthcare/value-analysis depth (described, not present). The framing of DHW as a complementary three-person team came through clearly, with Bill's question "is it just those two of you?" landing early enough to shape how the rest of the conversation positioned scale.

DHW illustrated the work with four cases: the non-invasive retinal glucose monitor (which prompted Bill's recognition of PSI's ophthalmic-instrumentation heritage and an unprompted "they should hire PSI" — a meaningful unsolicited signal), the Israeli skin-cancer body scanner, the 150-year-old family textile business teaching innovation thinking, and the vascularized bone-transplant company illustrating the lived-reality interview method. Eric layered in a fifth — the abdominal-imaging robotic platform at MGB — to teach the value-analysis-committee discipline and the tiered access strategy.

Bill's questioning was the engine of the meeting. He worked through DHW's offering in steps: do you define paths forward → how do you get access to clinicians → is it just qualitative or does it size markets → is it tech assessment or does it support fundraising → is it beyond that? His questions were not skeptical, they were the questions of someone building a working mental model and pressure-testing fit. Each one pulled more of DHW's method into view: the six-interviews-per-segment rule, the three-act interview structure, the indication-of-interest artifact, the Bayesian-prior framing.

Julia spoke less but pivoted the conversation at two important moments: when she raised TABA funding (revealing the SBIR commercialization-plan funding lens PSI uses) and when she surfaced the spin-out posture ("how broke into that though, to the spin-out idea"), which led Bill to disclose that PSI is an S-corp and cannot take outside investment, so any VC-funded technology must be spun out. That structural constraint had not surfaced in the May 7 conversation and is foundational to how any engagement gets shaped.

Bill closed the meeting by reframing the scope question: "Nick has shown you a very small sliver. Assuming it's PSI resources, you know, we would want to open it up to the aperture some." This was the most important sentence in the meeting. It moved DHW from "evaluate these two technologies" to "look across the portfolio." Whether that is a real scope expansion or an instinctive widening before the executive team has internally agreed, is the open question.

## What was unspoken

- **PSI is hiring DHW into a category it has used before, with mixed results.** Bill's disclosure of Wellesley Associates (single-person consultant, mixed-result interviews, PowerPoint deliverable) and Adam Ehrlich / Igenico (Gary's circle, TABA-funded interviewer) sets a benchmark. DHW is being compared to those engagements, even if Bill says "maybe not with the experience level that you have." The unspoken question is: does the cost-and-depth differential between DHW and a TABA-scale consultant justify itself, when PSI's default frame for this work is the TABA budget?

- **PSI's commercialization muscle is institutionally weak.** Julia named it plainly — "we frankly don't have a lot of experience raising capital for something like a spin out." Bill named the S-corp constraint as a structural blocker. Nick's earlier "valley of death" framing in the May 7 conversation now has a concrete explanation: PSI does not have, and arguably cannot have under its current structure, an internal commercialization team. This is not a knowledge gap to fix; it is a structural condition that shapes every engagement design.

- **Who actually decides is still unclear.** Nick is the relationship; Bill ran the conversation; Julia holds Optical Systems Division authority and made the structural disclosures. The internal alignment question — which of them must say yes, and on what — was deferred to "the three of us need to sit down and have an internal conversation."

- **The TABA frame is a budget frame, not a method frame.** Bill walked through TABA Phase 1 (6.5K), Phase 2 (50K), and noted DermVisio and TVisio are past the SBIR window so any spend is "PSI dollars out of the PSI overhead or bank account." This is the cost ceiling PSI is mentally anchored on. DHW's offer needs to either fit that ceiling, justify exceeding it materially, or be structured against milestones that defer the cash question — which is exactly the hybrid Option B the pre-meeting memo proposed, though that compensation conversation was deliberately not raised in this call.

- **Bill's "very small sliver" remark is also a signal of intra-PSI politics.** Nick is in the Biomedical Imaging division; Bill leads Applied Optics; Julia leads Optical Systems. Bill's instinct to broaden the aperture may reflect his view that the biomedical sliver is not the highest-value portfolio piece, or it may simply be due diligence. Either way, it means the scoping conversation is now an internal PSI conversation as much as a DHW conversation.

- **"Personal chemistry is #1" landed.** Daniel's late line and Bill's agreement ("that's why I think it's important actually to get in the building here") names the criterion PSI is actually evaluating against — fit, trust, and whether they want to work with us — independent of the methodology details. Bill's site-visit invitation is the real next step, not the email.

## Verbatim quotes that can change mindsets

These are the moments where language crystallized. They are the lines worth carrying forward into the email reflection and the proposal.

> "These technologies may now have more possible applications than PSI can realistically pursue all at once."
> — Daniel (and echoed in the pre-meeting memo). PSI did not push back. The prioritization framing is in.

> "We frankly don't have a lot of experience raising capital for something like a spin out."
> — Julia. The single most candid sentence in the call. Names PSI's actual gap, with no defensiveness.

> "We cannot take outside money into PSI itself... if we want to take VC money, we have to spin something out. Not that people would want to invest directly in PSI anyway."
> — Bill. The S-corp constraint plus a moment of self-deprecation that reveals how PSI views its own investability. Both halves matter.

> "Nick has shown you a very small sliver. Assuming it's PSI resources, you know, we would want to open it up to the aperture some."
> — Bill. The scope-widening signal. The pre-meeting hunch ("the constraint may be prioritization uncertainty") just got direct confirmation.

> "They should hire PSI and Nick specifically, because we have a long heritage in ophthalmic instrumentation."
> — Bill, on the glucose-monitor example. An unprompted offer of PSI's identity as a contract manufacturer / partner, not just as a technology owner. Worth noting — it may inform how DHW positions PSI to acquirers or licensees later.

> "Personal chemistry is #1. You know, it's like you have to, so that has to be there. And then other stuff, of course, has to be there too... Right. That's why I think it's important actually to get in the building here as well."
> — Daniel and Bill. The closest the meeting came to a mutual commitment.

> "We want to keep the momentum and get something done, not linger too long."
> — Nick. The pacing signal. After June 3 SBIR deadlines and Nick's June 7-9 Atlanta travel, the window is mid-June onward.
