# The outcome first, then the measures

**For: the sustaining portfolio scoreboard, Phase 2 · August 2026**

## The outcome we are driving

Leaders make significantly better decisions about the sustaining portfolio. Not a better report. Not more visibility for its own sake. The last monthly report ran for two and a half years and was paused without a ripple, which settled the question of whether reporting alone is the outcome.

Better decisions means, concretely: the eight recurring decisions the quarterly review owns. Which projects stop consuming resources. Where constrained capacity moves next quarter, and at whose expense. When quiet coping across business units stops being acceptable. Whether workload evidence justifies backfill. The annual cost-improvement commitment. Which known time-bombs get funded proactively. Which proven practices become the standard. And whether what the room decided last quarter actually happened.

That list is a draft, deliberately. Correcting it is the first piece of work, and it takes ten minutes of the right people's time.

## The relevant data

Whatever changes one of those eight decisions. Nothing else, no matter how easy it is to count. Twelve candidate measures are on the table, drawn from what stakeholders across the organization said they need. Each one is tested two ways:

1. Which decision would this measure change? A measure that changes none of them is documented and retired, regardless of how good its data is.
2. Can it be produced from data that exists today, and does that data hold up? Reachable now, reachable with a defined step, or blocked with cause. A documented "blocked" is a real finding, not a failure.

The test runs in reverse too. A decision on the list that no candidate serves tells us what the candidate list still owes. New measures get named and tested the same way, including any that come from this conversation.

## The best way to show it

Trends before precision: better, worse, or no change, with the evidence behind each measure one click deep. Every measure on the dashboard carries its own case: why it is here, what feeds it, which decision it informs. Anything that cannot survive a drill-down question does not ship.

Two instruments, two jobs. A monthly dashboard leaders absorb asynchronously, modeled on what Interventional already proved works. A quarterly review that spends its hour only on what requires a decision from that room. The quarterly is not the old monthly at lower frequency; it answers a different question.

Dollar-denominated measures are the destination, not the starting claim. The path to them runs through a linkage that has never been tested end to end. Testing it, with a hand-trace of twenty real projects, is one of the first things the work does, and the result gets reported as it lands, with its honest range.

## Why this order

Every prior scoreboard attempt here went straight from idea to artifact, and each one arrived before its evidence. This work runs the other way: outcome, then decisions, then only the measures that serve them, proven against real data before anything is put in front of leadership. The alignment being asked for is not a detour from the plan. It is the plan's first step, and it is ready to run.
