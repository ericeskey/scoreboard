# 20 — Decision Inventory (draft for Matt to correct, Thursday)

**Purpose: the list of recurring decisions the quarterly room actually owns. This is the cut criterion for the twelve candidates: in Week 2, every measure gets mapped to the decisions it could change, and a measure that maps to nothing is out, regardless of how reachable its data is. Drafted from what the organization has already said; Matt corrects it in ten minutes and the correction is the value.**

## Decisions the quarterly room owns (draft)

1. **Continue, pause, or kill.** Which sustaining projects stop consuming resources. (The reconciliation gap: savings claimed against canceled projects shows what happens when nobody owns this decision in a forum.)
2. **Resource shifts across business units.** Where constrained engineering and regulatory capacity moves next quarter, and at whose expense.
3. **Escalation of absorbed cross-BU impact.** When quiet coping stops being acceptable: the organization absorbs cross-BU problems well; the room decides what should no longer be absorbed. (The Lidocaine pattern: real impact, no metric caught it.)
4. **Backfill and capacity commitments.** Whether workload evidence justifies filling open roles or shifting scope instead.
5. **Cost-improvement target commitment.** Agreeing the annual target and the gap-closing actions when the pipeline underruns it (this year's target was still unagreed in July).
6. **Obsolescence and supplier-risk posture.** Which known time-bombs get funded proactively versus accepted as reactive risk.
7. **Standard-setting.** Whether a practice proven in one BU (the Interventional dashboard, Planview discipline) becomes the RemainCo standard, and on what schedule.
8. **Prior-commitment accountability.** Reviewing what the room decided last quarter and whether it happened: the item that makes the forum self-enforcing.

## What the room does NOT own (draft)

- Individual project management decisions (BU project leads own those).
- Monthly status absorption (the dashboards own that).
- Build/tooling choices (the build owner and IT own those, against the requirements baseline).

## How this gets used

Week 2: each of the twelve candidate measures is scored against this inventory. The question per Hubbard's discipline: what decision would this measurement change? Measures informing decisions 1 through 8 compete for the headline set; measures informing none are documented and retired from candidacy.
