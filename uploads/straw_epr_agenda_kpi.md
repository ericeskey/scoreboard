# STRAW — Quarterly Sustaining EPR: Agenda + KPI Set

**⚠️ This is a straw model — a provocation to react to, not a deliverable.** Built to make our thinking visible and shape scope. The real version gets built *with* Matt's BU leaders, mapped to Teleflex's actual data, and tested against who's in the EPR room. That validated build is Phase 1 of the engagement — this page is the sketch that proves we know how.

**Designed to answer the three decisions Matt named:**
1. Are we getting **better, getting worse, or no change**? (the direction his prior leadership always wanted, never got)
2. What are the **upcoming portfolio risks**? (anticipation, not history)
3. Is there **capacity to take on additional margin-improvement projects**? (sustaining as value-creation capacity, not just cost)

**Design principles:** measure *exposure*, not activity. Every KPI carries a **direction arrow** (▲ better / ▼ worse / ▬ flat) so the whole review reads as a trend story. Financials are the destination, not the starting line — see the roadmap band. Each KPI tagged **[Now]** (reachable from data you have) or **[Build]** (needs the impacted-SKU → SAP linkage or an EPPM source that doesn't exist yet).

---

## The quarterly EPR agenda (~60 min, decision-forcing — not a data dump)

1. **Portfolio health headline (5 min).** One slide. Better / worse / no-change across the domains below, as RAG + trend arrows vs. last quarter. The "are we improving?" answer, up front.
2. **Upcoming risks & exceptions (15 min).** What's *coming* — the fires visible this quarter before they hit. By-exception only: projects/product lines trending to trouble, supplier & obsolescence exposure, compliance-linked risk. Not a status readout of everything.
3. **Capacity & margin-improvement (15 min).** How much sustaining capacity is consumed by reactive/keep-the-lights-on work vs. available for proactive margin-improvement — and what that means for taking on more. The value-creation conversation.
4. **Decisions needed from leadership (15 min).** Explicit asks: where leadership support, reprioritization, or resource arbitration is required. The part that makes the meeting un-pausable — leaders come because decisions get made here.
5. **What's changing in how we measure (10 min).** The roadmap toward financial KPIs (the SKU→SAP linkage), so Harsh sees dollars coming and the review visibly improves each quarter.

## The KPI set (grouped in bounded domains both BUs and supply chain can read)

### A. Direction & Delivery — *"are we getting better?"* (Decision 1)
- **Portfolio completion velocity** — completed vs. newly-opened, trend over trailing quarters. ▲▼▬ [Now]
- **Aging / backlog trend** — share of portfolio past expected cycle time; is the tail growing? ▲▼▬ [Now]
- **Cycle-time trend** by project class (not raw counts — counts are what leaders already reject). ▲▼▬ [Now, if project dates are clean]
- **Recurrence rate** — sustaining issues that come back after being "closed" (a strain signal that hides in counts). ▲▼▬ [Now/Build]

### B. Risk & Anticipation — *"what's coming?"* (Decision 2)
- **At-risk project exposure** — risk-weighted, not headcount; which projects are trending to miss and what they'd hit. ▲▼▬ [Now/Build]
- **Supplier & component-obsolescence exposure** — concentration and last-time-buy horizon; the classic slow fire. ▲▼▬ [Now]
- **Compliance / CAPA-linked risk** — open CAPAs, aging deviations, NCR recurrence tied to portfolio (leading indicators of strain; the suppressed-impact signals). ▲▼▬ [Now]
- **Absorbed-impact watch** — logged coping (expedites, exclude-and-ship, line-down cross-training) as an early-warning count, not proof nothing broke. ▲▼▬ [Build]

### C. Capacity & Value — *"can we take on more margin work?"* (Decision 3)
- **Reactive vs. proactive load split** — % of sustaining capacity consumed keeping the lights on vs. available for improvement. ▲▼▬ [Now/Build]
- **Margin-improvement pipeline** — projects in flight and queued that improve product margin; the value-creation story. ▲▼▬ [Build]
- **Realized margin improvement** — delivered savings/margin from completed sustaining work (Harsh's financial bridge starts here). ▲▼▬ [Build]

### D. Financial (the destination — roadmap, not quarter-one)
- **Cost-of-sustaining** and **impacted-SKU → revenue-at-risk** — the KPIs Harsh wants. **[Build]** — gated on the impacted-SKU→SAP linkage. Named explicitly as the roadmap so leadership sees dollars coming; a single BU with reachable SKU data is the candidate beachhead to prove it first.

---

## How to read this on the call

- The **[Now]** KPIs are what makes a *credible first quarterly EPR possible fast* — enough to tell the better/worse/no-change story and answer the risk + capacity questions without waiting on data that doesn't exist.
- The **[Build]** KPIs are the roadmap — including Harsh's financials — and define the later phases of work.
- The **agenda item 4 (decisions needed)** is the single most important design choice: it's what converts the EPR from a report leaders ignore into a room where things get decided. That's the anti-"nobody noticed it was paused" mechanism.
- What's *not* here on purpose: raw project counts as a headline metric (dead on arrival), and any tooling/Power-BI discussion (that's downstream of settling what to measure).
