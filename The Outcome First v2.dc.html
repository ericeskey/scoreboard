The relevant data

Whatever changes one of those eight decisions. Nothing else, however easy it is to count. Twelve candidates are on the table, and each is tested twice.

Three of these eight decisions have no owner today

The decisions Harsh’s 2025 “Big Questions” were driving at, made explicit.

The list is the yardstick for every candidate measure: one that would not change any of these gets documented and retired. The line under each decision is where it gets settled today.

01

Continue, pause, or kill

Which sustaining projects stop consuming resources?

Today: no forum. Savings are still claimed against canceled projects.
02

Resource shifts across business units

Where does constrained engineering and regulatory capacity move next quarter, and at whose expense?

Today: settled business unit to business unit, case by case.
03

Escalation of absorbed impact

What should no longer be absorbed quietly across business units?

Today: absorbed, not decided. Real impact, and no measure caught it.
04

Backfill and capacity commitments

Does workload evidence justify filling open roles, or shifting scope instead?

Today: argued role by role, without workload evidence.
05

Cost-improvement target commitment

What is the annual target, and what closes the gap when the pipeline underruns it?

Today: this year's target was still unagreed in July.
06

Obsolescence and supplier-risk posture

Which known time-bombs get funded proactively, and which are accepted as reactive risk?

Today: decided when a supplier forces the timing.
07

Standard-setting

Does a practice proven in one business unit become the RemainCo standard, and on what schedule?

Today: spreads by example, or does not spread.
08

Prior-commitment accountability

What did the room decide last quarter, and did it happen? The item that makes the forum self-enforcing.

Today: revisited informally, when someone remembers to ask.
Not owned here
Individual project management decisions. Business-unit leads own those.
Monthly status absorption. The dashboards own that.
Build and tooling choices. The build owner and IT own those.
