The outcome first, then the measures

Every prior scoreboard attempt here went straight from idea to artifact. This one runs the other way: outcome, then decisions, then only the measures that serve them.

The outcome we are driving

Leaders make significantly better decisions about the sustaining portfolio. Not a better report. Not more visibility for its own sake. The last monthly report ran for two and a half years and was paused without a ripple, which settled the question of whether reporting alone is the outcome.

Better decisions means, concretely: the eight recurring decisions the quarterly review owns, from which projects stop consuming resources through to whether what the room decided last quarter actually happened. All eight are set out on page 2. That list is a draft, deliberately. Correcting it is the first piece of work, and it takes ten minutes of the right people’s time.

The relevant data

Whatever changes one of those eight decisions. Nothing else, no matter how easy it is to count. Twelve candidate measures are on the table, drawn from what stakeholders across the organization said they need. Each one is tested two ways.

  1. Which decision would this measure change? A measure that changes none of them is documented and retired, regardless of how good its data is.
  2. Can it be produced from data that exists today, and does that data hold up? Reachable now, reachable with a defined step, or blocked with cause. A documented "blocked" is a real finding, not a failure.

The test runs in reverse too. A decision on the list that no candidate serves tells us what the candidate list still owes. New measures get named and tested the same way, including any that come from this conversation. The twelve and their draft verdicts are on page 3.

The best way to show it

Trends before precision: better, worse, or no change, with the evidence behind each measure one click deep. Every measure on the dashboard carries its own case: why it is here, what feeds it, which decision it informs. Anything that cannot survive a drill-down question does not ship.

Two instruments, two jobs. A monthly dashboard leaders absorb asynchronously, modeled on what Interventional already proved works. A quarterly review that spends its hour only on what requires a decision from that room. The quarterly is not the old monthly at lower frequency; it answers a different question.

Dollar-denominated measures are the destination, not the starting claim, and the path to them is set out on page 3.

Where we want your thinking

Is “leaders make significantly better decisions” the outcome you would name, or a proxy for something you would state differently. If the outcome is wrong, everything downstream of it is wrong, and this is the cheapest moment to say so. Mark up this page, or take ten minutes with Matt, and we work from your version.

The eight decisions the quarterly room owns

Assembled from what people across the organization already said this forum should settle. Still a draft. It is the yardstick for every candidate measure: a measure that would not change one of these gets documented and retired. Correct the list and the measure set changes with it.

01

Continue, pause, or kill

Which sustaining projects stop consuming resources. Savings still claimed against canceled projects is what happens when no forum owns this.

02

Resource shifts across business units

Where constrained engineering and regulatory capacity moves next quarter, and at whose expense.

03

Escalation of absorbed impact

The organization absorbs cross-BU problems well. The room decides what should no longer be absorbed quietly.

04

Backfill and capacity commitments

Whether workload evidence justifies filling open roles, or shifting scope instead.

05

Cost-improvement target commitment

Agreeing the annual target, and the gap-closing actions when the pipeline underruns it.

06

Obsolescence and supplier-risk posture

Which known time-bombs get funded proactively versus accepted as reactive risk.

07

Standard-setting

Whether a practice proven in one business unit becomes the RemainCo standard, and on what schedule.

08

Prior-commitment accountability

Reviewing what the room decided last quarter and whether it happened. The item that makes the forum self-enforcing.

What the room does not own

  • Individual project management decisions. Business-unit project leads own those.
  • Monthly status absorption. The dashboards own that.
  • Build and tooling choices. The build owner and IT own those.

How the list gets used

Each of the twelve candidate measures is scored against these eight. Measures that inform one or more compete for the headline set. Measures that inform none are written down as retired, with the reason, so the same idea does not return next quarter as a new one.

Where we want your thinking

Which of these eight does your room genuinely own, and which belong to someone else. What is missing that you expect to decide in that hour. And which one, got right every quarter, would matter most to you.
